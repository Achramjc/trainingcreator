Standard Operating Procedure: Calibrated Torque Application and Witness Stamping on Primary Structure Fasteners

Document Number: SOP-QC-303
Version: 2.1
Effective Date: 04/14/2026
Department: Final Assembly

1.0 PURPOSE
This procedure defines the requirements for applying calibrated torque to primary
structure fasteners and for witness stamping the completed torque application, so that
every safety-of-flight fastener has objective, traceable evidence of correct installation
in accordance with the AS9100 quality management system and the applicable engineering
drawing.

2.0 SCOPE
This procedure applies to all primary structure fastener installations identified as
Class 1 on the engineering drawing, and to all Assembly Mechanics, Torque Technicians, and
Quality Inspectors performing or witnessing torque application on the wing spar and
fuselage frame assemblies. Non-structural fasteners follow SOP-QC-118.

3.0 DEFINITIONS
Class 1 Fastener: A fastener identified on the engineering drawing as safety-of-flight critical, requiring calibrated torque and independent witness verification.
Torque Wrench Calibration Interval: The maximum 90-day period a torque wrench may be used before recalibration is required.
Witness Stamp: The unique inspector stamp applied next to a torque application to record independent verification that the specified torque was achieved.
Torque Sequence: The engineering-specified order in which multiple fasteners in a joint must be tightened to achieve even clamp-up.

4.0 RESPONSIBILITIES
- Assembly Mechanics: Install fasteners and apply torque per the drawing callout
- Torque Technicians: Verify torque wrench calibration status before each use
- Quality Inspectors: Independently witness and stamp each Class 1 torque application
- Tool Crib: Maintain the torque wrench calibration schedule and pull wrenches from service when due

SAFETY WARNINGS
WARNING: Never use a torque wrench that is past its calibration due date. Verify the calibration sticker date before every torque application session.
CAUTION: Over-torquing a Class 1 fastener beyond 110 percent of the specified value requires fastener removal and replacement; do not attempt to back off and re-torque.
WARNING: Do not apply torque to a fastener in a joint with any adjacent fastener still loose, as this can induce uneven clamp-up and hidden structural damage.
DANGER: Do not work beneath a suspended wing spar assembly during torque application unless the overhead crane is locked out per SOP-EHS-011.

5.0 PROCEDURE
5.1 Verify Torque Wrench Calibration
Check the calibration sticker on the torque wrench and confirm the current date falls
within the 90-day calibration interval. Record the wrench serial number and calibration
due date on the traveler before use.

5.2 Verify Fastener and Joint Condition
Inspect the fastener, washer, and mating surfaces for damage, corrosion, or foreign object
debris. Confirm the correct fastener part number against the engineering drawing before
installation.
a. Confirm fastener grip length matches the joint thickness within drawing tolerance
b. Confirm the joint surfaces are free of sealant intrusion at the faying surface

5.3 Install Fasteners Finger-Tight in Sequence
Install all fasteners in the joint finger-tight, following the torque sequence diagram on
the drawing, before applying final torque to any single fastener.

5.4 Apply Calibrated Torque in Sequence
Apply torque to each fastener in the specified sequence to the drawing-called value of
28 Newton-meters, using a smooth continuous pull rather than a jerking motion. Do not skip
ahead in the sequence.
a. Apply the first pass at 50 percent of final torque across the full sequence
b. Apply the final pass at 100 percent of final torque across the full sequence

5.5 Verify Torque with a Check Wrench
Using a separate, independently calibrated check wrench, verify a sample of 20 percent of
the fasteners in the joint reach the specified 28 Newton-meter value without additional
rotation. Any fastener that rotates further under the check wrench fails and must be
re-torqued and re-verified.

5.6 Apply the Witness Stamp
The Quality Inspector independently observes torque application on any fastener not
covered by the check-wrench sample, then applies the individual witness stamp adjacent to
each completed fastener location on the traveler.

5.7 Record Torque Values on the Traveler
Record the achieved torque value, the torque wrench serial number, and the inspector
stamp number for each fastener location on the assembly traveler. Illegible or missing
entries require the torque application to be re-verified before the joint may be closed
out.

5.8 Apply Torque Stripe and Close Out the Joint
Apply the yellow torque stripe across each fastener head and the adjacent structure once
witness stamping is complete, providing a visual indicator that the fastener has not been
disturbed since verification. Close out the joint entry on the traveler.
