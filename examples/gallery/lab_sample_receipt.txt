Standard Operating Procedure: Clinical Sample Receipt, Accessioning, and Rejection Criteria

Document Number: SOP-LAB-055
Version: 5.0
Effective Date: 05/06/2026
Department: Clinical Laboratory

PURPOSE:
This Standard Operating Procedure establishes the process for receiving, accessioning,
and evaluating incoming patient specimens against defined rejection criteria, so that only
specimens suitable for accurate testing enter the testing workflow and every accessioning
decision is documented in accordance with CLIA and CAP requirements.

SCOPE:
This procedure applies to all specimens received at the central receiving bench, and to
all Accessioning Technicians and the on-duty Laboratory Supervisor. It applies to blood,
urine, and swab specimens; specialty specimens such as CSF follow SOP-LAB-071.

DEFINITIONS:
Accessioning: The process of assigning a unique laboratory identifier to a specimen and entering its associated order and patient information into the laboratory information system.
Quantity Not Sufficient (QNS): A specimen volume below the minimum required for the ordered test, typically less than 2 milliliters for a standard chemistry tube.
Hemolysis Index: A semi-quantitative measure of red blood cell breakdown in a serum or plasma sample, reported on a scale of 0 to 4.
Chain of Custody: The documented, unbroken record of specimen handling from collection through result reporting, required for legal or forensic testing.
Positive Patient Identification: Verification of patient identity using two independent identifiers, typically full name and date of birth.

RESPONSIBILITIES:
- Accessioning Technicians: Receive, inspect, and accession or reject incoming specimens
- Laboratory Supervisor: Adjudicate disputed rejections and approve redraw requests
- Phlebotomy Team: Recollect specimens rejected at accessioning
- Laboratory Director: Review the monthly specimen rejection rate report

SAFETY WARNINGS:
WARNING: Treat every specimen as a biohazard. Wear gloves and a face shield when opening any transport bag showing visible leakage.
CAUTION: Specimens received on dry ice must be handled with insulated gloves to prevent frostbite injury.
DANGER: Do not open a transport bag with a broken primary tube on the open bench. Move it to the biosafety cabinet before opening.
WARNING: Never pipette a specimen by mouth or attempt to recap a used needle found in a specimen bag; dispose of it directly into the sharps container.

PROCEDURE:

Step 1: Receive and Inspect the Specimen Bag
Inspect the outer transport bag for leakage, damage, or missing paperwork upon arrival at
the receiving bench. Any bag with visible leakage is handled per the biohazard spill
protocol in SOP-LAB-090 before further processing.

Step 2: Verify Positive Patient Identification
Confirm the specimen label carries two independent patient identifiers, typically full
name and date of birth, matching the accompanying requisition exactly. A mismatch between
the label and requisition requires rejection under the mislabeling criterion.

Step 3: Inspect Specimen Volume and Condition
Inspect the specimen for adequate volume, clotting in an anticoagulated tube, and gross
hemolysis. A chemistry tube with less than 2 milliliters of serum is rejected as Quantity
Not Sufficient.
a. Check anticoagulated tubes for visible clots by gentle inversion
b. Estimate the hemolysis index by comparing serum color to the reference card

Step 4: Verify Collection Time Against Test Stability
Compare the collection timestamp on the requisition to the current time and confirm the
elapsed time does not exceed the stability window for the ordered test, for example 2
hours for an ammonia level drawn on ice.

Step 5: Apply Rejection Criteria if Any Fail
Reject the specimen if any of the following apply: mislabeling, Quantity Not Sufficient,
clotted anticoagulated specimen, gross hemolysis incompatible with the ordered test, or
expired stability window. Record the specific rejection reason in the laboratory
information system.

Step 6: Notify the Ordering Location of a Rejection
Call the ordering unit or clinic to report the rejection and the specific reason within 30
minutes of the rejection decision, and document the name of the person notified and the
time of the call.

Step 7: Accession the Specimen
For a specimen passing all criteria, assign the unique accession number, enter the
patient, order, and specimen type information into the laboratory information system, and
print the aliquot labels required for testing.

Step 8: Sort and Route the Specimen
Sort the accessioned specimen to the correct testing section based on the ordered panel,
and place time-sensitive specimens, such as those requiring processing within 1 hour, in
the priority routing lane.

Step 9: Log the Chain of Custody for Applicable Specimens
For any specimen flagged for forensic or employment testing, complete the chain-of-custody
form at every handoff point, including receiving, accessioning, and routing, with a
signature and timestamp at each step.

Step 10: Reconcile the Daily Rejection Count
At the end of each shift, reconcile the total number of specimens rejected against the
laboratory information system rejection log and forward any discrepancy to the Laboratory
Supervisor before shift handoff.
