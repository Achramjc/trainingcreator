Standard Operating Procedure: Device History Record Compilation and Lot Release Review

Document Number: SOP-QA-014
Version: 3.2
Effective Date: 02/10/2026
Department: Quality Assurance

PURPOSE:
This Standard Operating Procedure establishes the method for compiling, reviewing, and
approving the Device History Record (DHR) for each production lot of the Model 220
Infusion Set prior to lot release, so that objective evidence exists that every unit was
manufactured in accordance with the Device Master Record (DMR) as required by 21 CFR
820.184 and ISO 13485 Section 7.5.8.

SCOPE:
This procedure applies to all finished-goods lots of the Model 220 Infusion Set built at
the Building 3 assembly line, and to all Quality Engineers, Assembly Technicians, and QA
Release Reviewers involved in compiling or approving the DHR. It does not cover component
incoming inspection records, which are governed by SOP-QA-006.

DEFINITIONS:
Device History Record (DHR): The compiled set of records containing the production history of a specific finished device lot, including all in-process and final inspection results.
Device Master Record (DMR): The compiled set of records containing the procedures and specifications for a finished device, including drawings, work instructions, and acceptance criteria.
Lot: A defined quantity of finished units, not to exceed 500 units, manufactured under essentially the same conditions and assigned a single lot number.
Nonconforming Material Report (NCMR): The controlled form used to document, disposition, and track any unit or component that fails to meet an acceptance criterion.

RESPONSIBILITIES:
- Assembly Technicians: Complete traveler entries and in-process checks at each station
- Quality Engineers: Verify test records and reconcile component lot traceability
- QA Release Reviewer: Perform the final DHR review and sign the lot release disposition
- Document Control: Archive the approved DHR for the 7-year retention period

SAFETY WARNINGS:
WARNING: Do not release a lot for shipment until every line of the DHR checklist has been
reviewed and initialed. A missing signature is treated as an open nonconformance.
CAUTION: Components awaiting DHR review are electrostatic-discharge sensitive. Handle only
at a grounded workstation wearing a wrist strap.
WARNING: A lot with an open NCMR of any severity may not proceed to release review until
the NCMR is closed and its disposition is recorded in the DHR.

PROCEDURE:

Step 1: Retrieve the Batch Traveler and Component Records
Pull the completed batch traveler for the lot from the production floor and confirm the
lot number, build date, and quantity built match the production schedule. Retrieve the
component lot certificates of conformance for every component listed on the traveler's
bill of materials.
a. Confirm the traveler carries a signature and date at every station
b. Confirm the quantity built does not exceed the 500-unit lot maximum

Step 2: Reconcile Component Lot Traceability
Cross-reference each component lot number recorded on the traveler against the incoming
inspection release records to confirm every component was released prior to use. Flag any
component whose incoming inspection record cannot be located.

Step 3: Verify In-Process Test Results
Review the in-process leak test results for the lot. Each unit must record a leak rate at
or below 0.5 mL per minute at the 300 mmHg test pressure specified in the DMR. Confirm the
test equipment calibration record was current on the test date.
a. Spot-check 10 percent of the recorded leak values against the raw data printout
b. Confirm the test technician's initials appear next to each recorded value

Step 4: Verify Final Inspection Results
Confirm the final visual and dimensional inspection was performed on a sample size of 32
units per the AQL 1.0 sampling plan, and that zero rejects were recorded. If any reject was
recorded, confirm an NCMR was opened and disposed before proceeding.

Step 5: Review Any Nonconforming Material Reports
Locate every NCMR referencing this lot number. Confirm each one carries a disposition of
use-as-is, rework, or scrap, an approval signature, and that any rework was verified by a
follow-up inspection recorded in the DHR.

Step 6: Verify Labeling and Packaging Records
Confirm the label reconciliation record shows the number of labels printed equals the
number of labels used plus the number voided, with no unaccounted-for labels. Confirm the
packaging record shows the correct catalog number and expiration date were applied.

Step 7: Complete the DHR Checklist
Complete the DHR compilation checklist, initialing each of the 14 line items to confirm the
corresponding record is present, legible, and internally consistent. Note the location of
the original record for any item stored outside the physical DHR binder.

Step 8: Perform the QA Release Review
The QA Release Reviewer independently reviews the compiled DHR against the checklist,
resolves any discrepancy with the Quality Engineer, and signs and dates the lot
disposition. Only a lot with a fully signed disposition may be transferred to finished
goods inventory.

REFERENCES:

- SOP-QA-009: Nonconforming Material Report Process
- 21 CFR 820.184: Device History Record
- ISO 13485:2016 Section 7.5.8: Traceability

RECORDS:
- Completed DHR binder, retained 7 years from lot release
- DHR Compilation Checklist (Form QA-014-A)
