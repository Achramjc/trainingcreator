Standard Operating Procedure: Monthly Eyewash Station Inspection

Version: 4.0
Effective Date: 05/11/2026
Department: Environmental Health and Safety

PURPOSE:
This procedure defines the monthly inspection and flushing of plumbed eyewash stations, so that every station delivers clean water at the required flow when it is needed.

SCOPE:
This procedure applies to all Facilities Technicians and the EHS Coordinator responsible for the plumbed eyewash stations in Buildings A, B and C.

DEFINITIONS:
Plumbed Eyewash Station: A station permanently connected to the potable water supply, fitted with dust covers and a stay-open valve.
Stay-Open Valve: The valve that remains open once actuated so the user needs no hands to keep the flow running.
Flushing: Running a station to clear stagnant water and confirm flow, recorded on the station tag.
Station Tag: The inspection tag attached to each station, carrying the date and initials of each monthly inspection.

RESPONSIBILITIES:
- Facilities Technicians: Inspect and flush each station and complete the station tag
- EHS Coordinator: Review the monthly inspection sheet and raise work orders for failures
- Area Supervisors: Keep the access path to each station clear
- Maintenance Planner: Schedule repairs raised against a failed station

SAFETY WARNINGS:
WARNING: Water from a station that has not been flushed for a month may be scalding or fouled. Stand clear of the bowl when actuating the valve.
WARNING: A station with an obstructed access path is a failed station, whatever its flow.
CAUTION: Wet floors around a flushed station are a slip hazard. Place a wet-floor sign before flushing.

PROCEDURE:

Step 1: Confirm the Access Path
Confirm the path to the station is clear for the full width of the aisle and that the station is visible from the work area it serves.

Step 2: Inspect the Dust Covers and Nozzles
Remove the dust covers and inspect both nozzles for scale, debris and damage. A blocked nozzle is a failed station.

Step 3: Actuate the Stay-Open Valve
Actuate the valve and confirm it stays open without being held. The valve must open in one second or less.

Step 4: Flush for Three Minutes
Flush the station for three minutes and watch the water clear. Water that is still discoloured after three minutes is a failed station.

Step 5: Check the Flow Pattern
Confirm both streams meet and rise to an equal height. An uneven pattern is recorded on the inspection sheet as a failure.

Step 6: Close the Valve and Refit the Covers
Close the stay-open valve, dry the bowl and refit both dust covers.

Step 7: Complete the Station Tag and Inspection Sheet
Date and initial the station tag, then record the result for each station on the monthly inspection sheet. The EHS Coordinator raises a work order for every failed station within one working day.
